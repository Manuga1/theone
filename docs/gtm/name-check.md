# Name check: can the project be called "Padma"?

Checked 2026-11-12 by web search only. **A web search is not a trademark clearance search.** It shows what is easy to find, not everything that exists. Nothing here is legal advice; a trademark lawyer should confirm before money is spent on a name.

Every finding is marked **verified** (a source I read today says it; link given) or **unverified** (could not be confirmed today).

## Verdict

**Usable with a second word; do not use "Padma" alone.** PADMA is a live, renewed US trademark for pharmaceutical and dietary-supplement products, owned by a Swiss herbal-medicine company that has sold in the US for decades and now belongs to a large Swiss healthcare group, so a health project called only "Padma" (or "Padma Health") invites confusion and an objection. I found no company using Padma for a research registry, health-data platform or research software, so a compound name such as "Padma Registry" sits in a different line of business, but only a USPTO search and a lawyer can confirm that.

### Confusingly similar health-data brands

- **datma** — a US federated real-world-data platform for healthcare. One letter and one syllable apart when spoken; it sells to the same kind of buyer (people who purchase health data). verified ([interview with its CEO](https://pulse2.com/datma-profile-noah-nasser-interview/amp/)).
- No other near-match (Padme, Parma, Palma, Padmed as a data brand) turned up in health data. unverified beyond these searches.

### Two practical drawbacks that are not legal

- "Padma" is a common given name and an ordinary Sanskrit word (lotus; the Canadian register records that translation). A one-word search for it returns people, the supplement, yoga studios and apps, so the project will be hard to find by name alone.
- A common word is a weak trademark for the project too: others can keep using it in their own fields.

## Three alternative forms

All three keep the name and add a word that says what the project is, which moves it away from supplements.

1. **Padma Registry** — says exactly what is live on day 4 (a contact registry). Domain to try first: padmaregistry.com.
2. **Padma Research** — broader, still fits once pilots run. Risk: "research" is generic and several firms worldwide may pair it with other names; check carefully. Domain: padmaresearch.com.
3. **Padma Cohorts** — names the thing buyers pay for. Least likely to collide with a wellness product. Domain: padmacohorts.com (not searched today).

Avoid: "Padma Health", "Padma Wellness", "Padma Labs", "Padma Bio" and anything with a lotus logo close to the supplement packaging.

Whatever form is chosen, the site copy can still say "Padma" in running text after the full name appears once; `BRAND.name` in `shared/brand.ts` is the single place to change it.

## Three things to do before buying a domain

1. **Run the free USPTO search yourself (20 minutes).**
   - Go to the USPTO trademark search at `tmsearch.uspto.gov`.
   - Search the wordmark `padma`. Filter to **Live** marks.
   - Read every live result's goods and services, especially in classes 5 (medicines, supplements), 9 (software), 35 (business and recruitment services), 42 (software as a service, research) and 44 (health services).
   - Repeat for the full form you intend to use (for example `padma registry`) and for sound-alikes (`padme`, `padmed`, `datma`).
   - Open any worrying record in the status system at `tsdr.uspto.gov` by its serial number (start with 75504144, 75115987, 75807059 and 97478431) to see the current owner and status.
   - Save a dated screenshot of the result list.
2. **Check the name where you will actually use it.** A registrar search for the chosen domain; the Tennessee Secretary of State business-name search for the name you would later form the company under; the Apple and Google app stores; and the handle on the two social networks you will use. Pick the form that is free in all of them.
3. **Ask a lawyer one question before spending more than the domain fee.** Use Wond'ry legal office hours or the first-conversation slot already planned after Gate B: "A Swiss supplement company holds PADMA in class 5 in the US. Is 'Padma Registry' (or the form chosen) for a research-participant registry and software safe to use and to file?" Until that answer, buy one inexpensive domain, print nothing in bulk, and keep the name in one constant so it can change in a day.

## Limits of this check

- Web search only; no USPTO, state or common-law database was queried directly.
- Trademark facts come from third-party sites that copy USPTO data and can be out of date.
- Domain status could not be tested from this session.
- Unregistered ("common-law") use by small local businesses does not reliably show up in search.
