# Questions for the first legal conversation, the ethics office and a purchasing administrator

**Draft for legal review. Not legal advice.** These are questions, not answers. They come from our legal research, which was not checked by a lawyer, and from the independent review of the plan.

## Part 1: the first one-hour legal conversation

When: after Gate B is passed (Friday 4 December), in the window 5 December to 10 January. Ask Wond'ry now about legal office hours or a mentor who is a lawyer, so the hour is booked before you need it.

Send this list two days ahead, with `founders-one-page.md`, `registry-notice.md`, `pilot-scope-one-page.md` and `letter-of-intent.md`.

Ask questions 1–6 first. They decide what you can do in January. Questions 7–15 matter before Padma holds any real health data; if time runs out, ask which of them need a second meeting.

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which entity should we form, and when? A Tennessee LLC (about $700 in its first year, by our research) or a corporation registered in Tennessee? Does forming in January really delay the first annual fee by a year? | A buyer cannot pay, and we cannot sign, until a company and a business bank account exist. An LLC is cheaper; a corporation may suit outside investment better |
| 2 | Does Vanderbilt, or the medical centre, have any claim on work done by students who are also lab employees or trainees? What must each founder sign to move their work into the company? Is anything needed from an earlier collaborator who has left? | University policy can give the university a student's work on a faculty-led project. The company must own the code, the list, the domain and the name cleanly |
| 3 | Until the company exists, the two founders hold the registry list personally. What are they personally liable for under the notice, and is the notice true and sufficient as written? | Federal consumer-protection law makes the promises in a privacy notice enforceable. Today they are enforceable against two students |
| 4 | Please review the one-page pilot proposal and the letter of intent. Are they safely non-binding before the company exists? What must the signed agreement add: liability cap, withdrawal, who owns the data, banned uses? | These are the first documents a buyer sees. A startup may want to sign in January |
| 5 | For Offer C, when can a startup's work honestly be treated as product feedback and not research? Who must make that call, and what should we ask them to put in writing? | If it is research and nobody has ethics cover, the data may be unusable and participants unprotected. Ethics review adds about $2,000–$6,000 and four weeks |
| 6 | May a first pilot invite registry members who live in, or are legal residents of, states other than Tennessee? Should any states be left out at the start (Washington, Nevada, Connecticut, Maryland, Illinois, Vermont were flagged in our research)? | Most students and many relatives are residents of other states. Some states have strict consumer-health-data laws with no size threshold |
| 7 | Is a flat referral fee from a lab to a student-owned company acceptable, and when does a per-person fee become a prohibited finder's fee? | Offer A is priced flat for this reason. We need to know where the line is before quoting anything else |
| 8 | For Padma's own data later: do we run our own protocol at an independent ethics board, or act as a vendor under a buyer's board? Which do academic and company buyers accept? | Pilot 1 gives us no reusable data. A reusable, recontactable dataset needs our own protocol. This is the step that makes the data ours to license |
| 9 | When we later license data with consent, is that a "sale" under California, Washington and other state laws, needing a signed authorisation? Can our design meet each law's definition of de-identified? | Calling it a licence does not change the law. Rich wearable data is hard to de-identify |
| 10 | Would Padma be a "vendor of personal health records" under the FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule, or a "provider of health care" under California's medical-information law, once it holds health data? | Either status brings notification duties and penalties that a small team must plan for before collecting anything |
| 11 | If participants are ever paid through Padma, or share in licence revenue: does that raise money-transmission, securities or undue-influence problems? How should tax forms work now that the reporting threshold is $2,000 per person? | The money rule keeps Padma out of participant payments today. The long-term idea is a revenue share, and it must be legal before it is promised |
| 12 | What must every priced email contain to meet commercial-email rules? Is our opt-out line and postal address enough? | Priced proposals go out from the Padma domain. Mistakes here are cheap to avoid |
| 13 | "Padma" is an existing brand in health products. Given the name-check result, can we use the name for research services, and should we add a second word? | Changing a name after printing, a domain and a company filing costs more than changing it now |
| 14 | If either founder is on a student visa: what work for Padma is allowed before and after the company exists? | Unpaid work on your own company can still count as employment. The international student office should be asked too |
| 15 | Before real health data: what must the privacy policy and consent say; what does sending free text to an outside AI service require; what insurance do we need; when will buyers ask for a security audit? | The current software must not hold real health data. These set the cost and order of the work to change that |

Things to tell the lawyer, so the hour is not spent on background:

- No company, no revenue, no participants, no health data. Budget under $500 until Gate B.
- The registry holds contact details only: name, email, age band, state, devices owned, study types. No advertising trackers.
- The software in the repo runs on simulated data only.
- The first buyers are funded startups. Universities are for discovery until the ethics office answers.
- Padma never holds participant money and never buys devices.

Low-cost routes our research found (confirm each): Wond'ry mentors include a lawyer; the law school's enterprise clinic serves low-income entrepreneurs and may not take student founders; a local startup firm offers a free first consultation; the state bar runs a referral service.

## Part 2: the research-ethics office

The three questions in `permission-emails.md` go first. Keep these for the reply or a follow-up call.

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which board reviews what: the university's social and behavioural board, or the medical centre's? Who answers for a registry that is not itself a study? | Our research found two boards. Asking the wrong one costs weeks |
| 2 | Is the notice Padma sends to registry members "recruitment material" that the board must approve word for word? | If yes, Padma sends only the lab's approved text and changes nothing |
| 3 | What does an amendment adding a recruitment source cost, and how long does it take? | Our research found amendment fees at the medical centre and no turnaround time. Both decide whether Offer A is worth a lab's effort |
| 4 | May the fee be flat per study? Is any per-person fee allowed? Must it be disclosed in the consent form? | Finder's fees are often restricted. Offer A is flat until you say otherwise |
| 5 | If founders only pass on a study notice and report who clicked, are they "engaged in research"? At what point would they need training and to be named as study staff? | The line decides what Padma may do in Offers A, B and D |
| 6 | For Offer D (reminders and logistics for a lab's existing participants): what would the lab need to file? | We ask about this offer in interviews only. We need to describe it correctly |
| 7 | Are students recruiting people they know a concern for undue influence? What wording or limits do you expect? | Personal contacts are the first channel. Labmates, mentees and anyone met as a patient are already excluded |
| 8 | Does either founder need to file a conflict-of-interest disclosure as a student, and with whom? | One founder works in a lab; the other is affiliated with the medical centre |
| 9 | Is the registry check-in email (a two-minute, non-health task sent 30 days after sign-up, to measure response) research, or routine upkeep of a list? | We want to measure whether members respond. We will not publish it as research |
| 10 | The founders plan to run a 14-day protocol on themselves and show their own data as a sample. Any concern? | It is two people's own data, shown by choice, not a study. Better to ask than to assume |

## Part 3: a purchasing administrator

The questions in `permission-emails.md` go first. Ask these on a call. Ask them separately at the university and at the medical centre: they are separate legal entities with separate purchasing.

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the small-purchase limit, and the purchasing-card limit? | A fee under the limit may need one signature and no bid |
| 2 | What does a new supplier have to provide: tax form, insurance, bank details, a conflict check? | We need to know what the company must have before it can be paid |
| 3 | How long does supplier set-up take in practice? | The plan assumes no university money before March |
| 4 | Are there extra rules when the owner is a current student or trainee of the institution? | Likely the hardest step. Better to know in November |
| 5 | Above what amount is a sole-source justification or competing quotes needed? | Sets a ceiling for a first deal |
| 6 | Can a federal grant pay an outside recruitment service? Under which cost category? | Many labs' only money is grant money |
| 7 | What are the usual payment terms after invoice? | A company with under $500 cannot wait long. Our one data point is a university payment to a founder that took more than six weeks |
| 8 | Who signs a small service agreement: the PI, the department, or a contracts office? Does the university insist on its own template? | Tells us whether to send our one-page scope or wait for theirs |
| 9 | How does the lab pay research participants today? | Confirms the money rule: the lab pays participants through its own system, not through Padma |
