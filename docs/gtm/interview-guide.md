# Interview guide

How to run a 20-minute discovery interview, what to ask each kind of person, and how to tell real demand from politeness.

## The one rule

Ask about what they did last time. Do not ask what they would do, and do not describe Padma until the last three minutes. People are kind about ideas and honest about their own past.

## The 20 minutes

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Thank them. Say: "I am a student doing discovery interviews. I am not selling anything today. I will take notes; I will not quote you by name." Ask if 20 minutes still works |
| 2–12 | The five buyer questions, in order. Follow up with "what happened next?" and "what did that cost?" |
| 12–16 | Archetype follow-ups (below). Pick two |
| 16–18 | The recontact question, if it has not come up |
| 18–19 | Only now, two sentences on Padma: "Padma is a registry of adults willing to be invited to studies, and a service that sends a research team's approved notice to the ones who opted in. It is a student project with no enrolled participants yet." Then stop talking |
| 19–20 | Ask for two names. Ask what they would want to see next, if anything. Thank them |

During the call:

- Talk less than a quarter of the time.
- Write down numbers and names exactly as said.
- Never quote a price. If asked, say: "We are still learning the right number. What did the last one cost you?"
- If they are at a university and ask for a quote, say: "I can send one once the ethics office has answered our questions."
- Never say "patients". Never mention a number from the simulated product.
- Do not show software unless they ask. If they ask, book a second call and use `demo-script-10min.md`.

## The five questions for every buyer

1. The last time you collected this kind of data, what did it cost per participant, all-in?
2. Where did it break?
3. Which budget paid, and who approved it?
4. Have you paid an outside vendor? Why that one?
5. When did you last need one more measurement from the same people, and what was it worth?

For startups, add: do you hold an approved protocol, or is this product feedback?

## Follow-ups by archetype

### Academic PI

- How many people did you need, and how many weeks did it take to get them?
- Do you use ResearchMatch? What does it not give you?
- Who is hardest for you to recruit?
- If you added a new recruitment source, who files the amendment and how long does it take?
- Is there a study starting in the next six months where recruiting worries you?

### Study coordinator or lab manager

- Walk me through the last participant you enrolled, from first contact to consent. Where did time go?
- How many people do you contact for each one who finishes?
- Which tools do you use for screening and reminders?
- When the lab buys a small outside service, who signs and how long does it take?
- What do you do when someone stops responding halfway?

### Funded startup founder or clinical lead

- Do you hold an approved protocol, or is this product feedback? Who made that call?
- How did you find the people in your last validation study? What did each one cost?
- Which money paid for it: a grant line, or operating budget?
- How fast can you approve a four-figure spend, and how do you pay small suppliers?
- If you could re-measure the same users in three months, what would you measure?

### Recruitment-infrastructure staff (ResearchMatch, REDCap, MyCap, recruitment support)

- Which kinds of study still struggle to enrol with the tools you provide?
- What do labs ask you for that you cannot offer?
- How do labs here bring in an outside recruitment source? What goes wrong?
- What should a student project not duplicate?
- Who else should I talk to?

### Privacy or ethics scholar

- What can "de-identified" honestly mean for wearable or daily-log data?
- What would a fair consent to be contacted again say? What do people misunderstand?
- Where do peer-run registries go wrong?
- Which promise in our notice would you remove? (Send them `registry-notice.md` beforehand only if they ask.)
- What would make you tell a student to stop?

### Former participant-data-company employee

- What did the first three customers actually buy?
- How were follow-up studies with the same people priced, compared with the first study?
- Why did buyers leave?
- What did it cost to keep a member responsive for a year?
- What did you promise members that you later regretted?

Ask for general patterns only. Do not ask for anything confidential.

### Pharma evidence staff (interview only)

- How does your team decide to pay for data collected directly from research participants?
- Who signs, and how long does supplier approval take?
- What would a small supplier have to prove before you would talk to them?
- When did you last go back to the same people? Who arranged it?

Do not pitch. Do not ask for a pilot. This call is for learning how the far end of the market buys.

### Prospective participant (ten people, most over 30)

Do not ask about their health. Do not ask what conditions they have.

- Have you ever taken part in a research study? What was it like to sign up?
- Would you do a 14-day study with a short daily check-in and a few photos of meals? What would make it not worth it? (This is a question, not an offer.)
- What amount would make 14 days fair for your time? (Write down the number. Promise nothing.)
- What would worry you about joining a list like this?
- Would you be willing to be asked again a few months later by the same team?
- Who would you want to know is behind it before you signed up?

## The three thesis tests

Padma's thesis: buyers pay more for people they can go back to. Three separate tests.

### 1. Demand (by interview)

Ask every buyer, in these words:

> "When did you last go back to the same participants? What did it cost? What would you have paid?"

Record three things: the occasion (what they needed), the cost (money and weeks), and the number they say they would have paid. If they have never gone back, record that; it counts against the thesis.

### 2. Supply (free, measured)

Do not ask this in interviews. It is measured: 30 days after sign-up, every registry member gets a two-minute non-health task by email. Target: half respond within 7 days. See `recontact-test.md`.

In participant interviews, ask only: "Would you be willing to be asked again a few months later?" and write down the answer. An answer is not a measurement.

### 3. Price (on every quote)

Recontact is an optional, separately priced line on every proposal. When you go through a proposal, ask:

> "Do you want the recontact line in or out?"

Then stop talking. One of the first three buyers buying it is a pass. A second buyer paying to re-measure people recruited for someone else is a strong pass.

## What counts as demand

| Counts | Does not count |
|---|---|
| They name a specific study and its budget | "This is really interesting" |
| They introduce whoever controls purchasing | "Send me a deck" |
| They ask for a written proposal | "Keep me posted" |
| | "We would definitely use this" |
| | An offer to "collaborate" with no study named |
| | An introduction to another student |

## Scoring, in one line

**Score = the number of "yes" answers among: named a study, named a budget, named a purchasing route, asked for a proposal (0 to 4).** Compliments score nothing. A prospect counts for Gate B only with a yes on the first three.

## Logging in the tracker

One row per person in the tracker (`tracker-template.csv`). Update it the same day.

| Column | What to enter |
|---|---|
| `id` | A running number. Never reuse one |
| `track` | `revenue`, `discovery`, `january_wave`, `interview_only`, `expert`, `participant` or `permission` |
| `organisation`, `person`, `role` | As on their official page |
| `source` | Where you found them: `targets.csv` row, registry ID, paper, or who introduced you |
| `warm_or_cold` | `warm` or `cold` |
| `owner` | Which of you owns the next step |
| `first_contact_date` | Date of the first message |
| `status` | `to_contact`, `sent`, `follow_up_1`, `follow_up_2`, `replied`, `call_booked`, `interviewed`, `proposal_requested`, `proposal_sent`, `declined`, `no_reply`, `do_not_contact` |
| `next_step`, `next_step_date` | One action, one date. Never blank unless the status is final |
| `interview_date` | Date of the call |
| `named_study` | The study's name, or `no` |
| `budget_named` | The amount or budget line they named, or `no` |
| `purchasing_route` | The person or process they named, or `no` |
| `asked_for_proposal` | `yes` or `no` |
| `recontact_interest` | `none`, `curious` or `wants_price` |
| `notes` | One line. Full notes live in your notes file, not here |

Do not put participant interviews' personal details in the tracker beyond a first name. Never record anything about anyone's health.

## Gates

| Gate | Date | Passed when |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Friday 20 November 2026 | Permissions answered or chased twice; 4 calls done or booked; 25 warm and 40 cold requests sent; 40 registry sign-ups |
| **B** | Friday 4 December 2026 | 8 buyer interviews logged, 3 of them startups; 1 prospect who named a study, a budget and a purchasing route. If there is none, change segment before January |
| **C** | Friday 12 February 2027 (startup); Friday 12 March 2027 (university) | Startup scope signed with fee deposit; or university amendment filed and purchase order requested |

On each gate day, sit together for 30 minutes, count from the tracker, and write the result at the top of the tracker's notes. Do not round up.
