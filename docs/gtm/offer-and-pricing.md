# Offer and pricing

**Every figure here is untested. The first five interviews are for learning the real number, not for defending these.**

Padma is a student project in pilot stage. It has no enrolled participants yet. Registry members are leads, not a cohort; each pilot still needs its own recruiting.

## The four offers and the recontact line

Exactly as in `plan.md`:

| Offer | Buyer | What they get | Starting fee | Ethics cover |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Referral campaign** | Academic labs | The lab's approved study notice sent to registry members who opted in to that kind of study; a referral report. Padma does not screen, consent or see study data | Flat $500–$1,500 per study | Lab files a minor amendment naming Padma as a recruitment source |
| **B. Pilot cohort** | Startups first; labs later | 15–25 completers on a simple protocol: surveys, logs, the participant's own wearable | $150–$300 per completer, service fee only | Buyer's approved protocol |
| **C. Design partner** | Funded startups | 10 participants, one protocol, weekly call | $2,500–$5,000 fixed | Only where the startup holds an approved protocol or its counsel treats the work as product feedback. Otherwise add ethics-review cost and four weeks |
| **D. Recontact operations** | Labs with a finished cohort | Reminders and logistics to get one more measurement from the lab's own participants | Ask in interviews only | Lab's protocol |
| **Recontact line** | Any | Optional, priced separately | $50–$150 per participant | Same protocol |

Devices (CGM, cuffs) wait for a second pilot. The buyer supplies them, under a protocol that covers safety and incidental findings.

## The money rule

1. Every quote shows two lines.
2. **Line 1, participant compensation and devices:** paid and shipped by the buyer directly. Padma never holds participant money and never buys devices.
3. **Line 2, Padma's service fee:** the only thing Padma invoices, and only through a company with a business bank account.
4. Until the company and the bank account exist, you sign nothing and accept no money. Scopes go out as proposals.
5. No price is quoted to a university lab until the ethics office answers.
6. Anything that quotes a price goes from the Padma domain, with an opt-out line and a postal address.

What to expect on timing (from `plan.md`): a funded startup can sign between mid-January and mid-February. A university cannot pay a new student-owned vendor before March.

## Who gets which offer, and when

| Who | Offer | When you may quote it |
|---|---|---|
| Funded startups (revenue track) | C first, then B | When they ask for a written proposal. As a proposal only until the company exists |
| Vanderbilt, VUMC and Meharry labs (discovery track) | A, as a free or at-cost trial with one lab | Only after the ethics office answers in writing |
| Outside labs (January wave) | A or B | From January, in the form their own ethics office accepts |
| Labs with a finished cohort | D | Not quoted. Ask in interviews: "Would you have paid for this on your last study?" |
| Pharma evidence teams, health-AI data buyers | None | Interview only |

## Why each fee is untested

| Offer | Where the starting number came from | Why it may be wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **A. $500–$1,500 flat per study** | Set flat because a per-head fee paid to students can look like a finder's fee, which many ethics offices restrict. Our research had suggested $100–$300 per enrolled participant; that estimate was unverified | Labs here already have ResearchMatch free. Setting up a new supplier may cost a department more effort than a small fee is worth. Nobody has told us what a referral is worth to them |
| **B. $150–$300 per completer, service fee only** | Labs pay participants roughly $250–$590 for multi-week wearable protocols (two verified examples). Our research estimated $600–$1,200 per completer all-in; that estimate was unverified. The service fee is what is left once the buyer pays compensation and devices directly | We have never run the protocol. We do not know the support minutes per participant or the completion rate. The founders' dry run gives the first real number |
| **C. $2,500–$5,000 fixed** | Research suggested $2,000–$10,000 for a paid design-partner pilot (unverified). Independent ethics review costs about $2,000–$6,000, so the offer only works when the startup already has cover | No startup has told us what budget line this comes from or how fast they can approve it |
| **D. Recontact operations** | No number at all | We do not know whether labs pay for this or do it with their own staff |
| **Recontact line, $50–$150 per participant** | A guess. It has no anchor | This is the thesis. No buyer has ever been asked. Nothing in our research gives a public price for going back to the same people |

Other anchors from the research, to test rather than repeat: online panels charge a platform fee of roughly 25–43% on top of participant rewards (verified from their pricing pages). Recruitment firms are said to charge about $100–$1,000 or more per enrolled participant (unverified).

## What to learn in the first five interviews

Write the answers in the tracker. Bring them to the Gate B review.

1. **All-in cost per participant** the last time they did this. Is our Offer B fee a small or large share of it?
2. **Who pays participants, and how.** Does "you pay participants directly" make their life easier or harder?
3. **The budget line and the approver.** Is there a threshold below which one person can say yes? Is our fee under it?
4. **Flat or per person.** Which do they find easier to approve: a flat fee, a per-completer fee, or a cap?
5. **Recontact.** The last time they went back to the same people: what it cost, and what they would have paid. Is $50–$150 laughable, fair or cheap?
6. **Ethics cover (startups).** Approved protocol, product feedback, or neither. If neither, who pays for review?
7. **What they would cut.** Which part of the offer do they not need?

## How the fee might be revised

Change one thing at a time, and only on evidence from at least three interviews.

| If you hear | Then |
|---|---|
| Three buyers say the Offer B fee is small next to their all-in cost | Raise the range. Quote the top of it next |
| Three buyers say a per-completer fee is hard to approve | Offer a fixed fee with a cap on completers |
| The ethics office allows only a flat fee, or only cost recovery | Offer A stays flat, or becomes a free trial with one lab for the reference |
| The ethics office allows a per-referral fee | Test it against the flat fee with the next two labs |
| A startup has no ethics cover and will not pay for review | Do not discount. Offer C waits, or the startup's counsel puts in writing that it is product feedback |
| The first three buyers all decline the recontact line | Ask why. Lower the price once. If the next three decline too, the thesis has failed its price test |
| A buyer asks for recontact before you offer it | Quote the top of the range and write down their exact words |
| The dry run shows support takes more than [x] minutes per participant per day | Recompute the Offer B fee from your real hours before the next quote |

## What is not for sale yet

- Any dataset, data licence or "access to the population". Padma holds no study data.
- The registry list or anyone's contact details. They are never sold. A study team receives them only when a member says yes to that study.
- Data from pilot 1 for reuse. It belongs to the buyer's study.
- Device studies (CGM, blood-pressure cuffs). Second pilot only, with devices supplied by the buyer.
- Medical records, lab results, genomics, imaging.
- Participants outside the US.
- "We run your study." Padma refers, reminds and reports. It does not screen or take consent. It handles study data only inside the buyer's tool, and only where the buyer's approved protocol names the founders as study staff.
- A per-head fee to a university lab, until the ethics office confirms it is allowed.
- The software as a licence.
- Anything for a banned use: advertising, insurance underwriting, employment, credit, housing, political targeting, law enforcement or immigration, re-identification, resale to data brokers, litigation against participants, gambling or behavioural-vulnerability targeting.
- A revenue share with participants. Nothing is licensed, so nothing is shared. Do not quote a split.
