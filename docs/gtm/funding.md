# Funding that does not cost ownership

Padma's budget until Gate B is under $500. Two steps after that cost real money: forming the company and independent ethics review. These are the options our research found for paying for them without giving up ownership.

Everything here came from web search results, not from the programmes themselves. Dates and amounts marked "not found" must be asked for. Confirm each fact with the programme before you rely on it.

## What the money pays for, in order

| Order | What | Rough cost | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Domain, mailbox on that domain, a postal address for priced emails | About $12 for the domain, about $7 a month for the mailbox, plus the address | Needed now. Paid from your own pocket |
| 2 | The company (a Tennessee LLC, by our research) | About $700 in its first year | Without it nobody can pay you and you can sign nothing. Form it after Gate B |
| 3 | A first hour with a lawyer, if no free route appears | Not known. Ask for a fixed fee | Decides the entity type and the first agreement |
| 4 | Independent ethics review of Padma's own protocol | About $2,000–$6,000 | This is the step that makes data Padma's to license. It comes after Gate C |
| 5 | Insurance, if a buyer or the lawyer says it is needed | Not known | Ask before the first signed pilot |

Do not spend grant money on devices, advertising or participant payments. Buyers supply devices and pay participants directly.

## The options

### 1. Chancellor's Launch Grants (Center for Entrepreneurship, Owen)

- **What our research found:** $250,000 a year in grants that take no ownership. Only Vanderbilt students may pitch. A startup that has raised $250,000 or more is not eligible. In the cycle held in November 2025, six of ten teams received $10,000 each, with a final round in spring 2026.
- **Not found:** the 2026–27 dates and deadlines.
- **Ask them:**
  1. When is the next cycle, and what is the application deadline?
  2. Must the company exist before we apply, or before the money is paid?
  3. Can the grant be paid to a company bank account instead of to a student personally?
  4. May the money be spent on forming the company and on independent ethics review?
  5. Which alumni founders in digital health would talk to two students for 20 minutes?
- **Who asks:** Maanas, this week. It is on the plan's list of first asks.

### 2. Wond'ry micro-grants

- **What our research found:** the Wond'ry has awarded $246,800 in micro-grants in total since autumn 2022. A micro-grant can open the path to the national I-Corps programme.
- **Not found:** the amount of a single award, and the application dates.
- **Ask them:**
  1. How much is one micro-grant, and what may it pay for?
  2. When is the next deadline, and who decides?
  3. Does a student project without a company qualify?
  4. May it pay for discovery costs: a domain, a mailbox, travel to interviews?
- **Who asks:** Maanas, at the Tuesday 17 November session (4:15pm).

### 3. The monthly pitch (Firestarter, Center for Entrepreneurship)

- **What our research found:** a monthly pitch competition open to the first ten student founders who apply each month.
- **Not found:** prize amounts and the next date.
- **What it is good for:** practice, feedback and being seen by the people who run the Launch Grants. Treat any prize as a bonus.
- **Ask them:**
  1. When does the next month's sign-up open?
  2. What is the format and the time limit?
  3. Is there a prize, and how is it paid?
- **Before you pitch:** use `deck-outline.md`. No simulated numbers, no prices, no earnings claims.

### 4. The regional I-Corps course

- **What our research found:** the Wond'ry runs a route into the National Science Foundation's I-Corps programme. The national programme carries a $50,000 grant from the NSF, not from the Wond'ry. The plan asks about the regional course, which is built around customer-discovery interviews: the work you are already doing.
- **Not found:** the next regional course dates, and whether a student team without a faculty lead qualifies.
- **Ask them:**
  1. When is the next regional course, and how do we apply?
  2. Do we need a faculty or technical lead on the team?
  3. Does the regional course pay any discovery costs?
  4. Does taking part create any university claim on what we build?
  5. What does a team need to show to go on to the national programme?
- **Who asks:** Maanas, at the Tuesday 17 November session.

## Rules for any application

- Say what is true: a student project in pilot stage, with no enrolled participants and no revenue.
- Give the real number of registry sign-ups and interviews on the day you apply. Nothing from the simulated product.
- Do not use the Vanderbilt name or logo in a way that suggests endorsement.
- If a grant is paid to one of you personally, record it in `founders-one-page.md` (section 9) and ask the lawyer how to move it into the company.
- A grant from the university may come with conditions on ownership or reporting. Read them before accepting, and ask the lawyer if unsure.
