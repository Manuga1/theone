# Guardrails

The rules that keep two students, the registry members and the idea out of trouble. Read this before the alignment call. If something you are about to do is not clearly allowed here, ask the other founder first.

This is not legal advice. The legal points come from research that no lawyer has checked.

## 1. Stages: what Padma may hold and do

Padma is at stage 0. You move up a stage only when every line in "Before you start" is true.

| Stage | What it is | What Padma holds | Allowed | Before you start |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Now** | Discovery and the registry | Contact details only: name, email, age band, state, devices owned, study types | Interviews. Permission emails. The registry, to personal contacts. Proposals and letters of intent, unsigned by you. The simulated demo | Alignment call held. Notice published with both names. Forms tested |
| **1. First pilot** | A paid service under the buyer's approved protocol | The registry, and counts for reports. No copy of study data: it stays in the buyer's tool | Sending the buyer's approved notice. Study-specific recruiting. Reminders and logistics only as the buyer's protocol allows. Invoicing the service fee | Gate B passed. Company and business bank account exist. First legal conversation held. Buyer's ethics cover in writing. For a university: the ethics office's written answer |
| **2. Padma's own protocol** | Padma collects survey, log and wearable data it may later license | Health data from consenting participants | Collection under Padma's own approved consent. Licensing study by study with permission | Gate C passed. Protocol and consent approved by an independent ethics board (about $2,000–$6,000). Lawyer's advice on state laws. Every line in section 6 |
| **3. Later** | Medical records, lab results, genomics, imaging | Sensitive data with extra legal rules | Only with specialist advice | Stage 2 running well. Specialist legal advice. Insurance. A security audit if buyers ask |

Devices (glucose monitors, blood-pressure cuffs) are a second pilot inside stage 1. The buyer supplies them, under a protocol that covers safety and incidental findings.

Our legal research numbers its stages slightly differently: it puts Padma's own low-risk collection before the first paid collection. The plan puts the first paid pilot first, under the buyer's protocol, because that needs no data in Padma's hands. This table follows the plan.

**What pilot 1 does not give you:** any data you may reuse, license or show another buyer. It gives a paying reference, operating experience and a tested recruiting channel.

## 2. Conflicts

1. Neither of you sells to, or takes money from, your own lab, your supervisor or anyone who grades or evaluates you. Those people may be interviewed and may introduce you. Nothing more.
2. No recruiting labmates, people you supervise, or students you teach or mentor.
3. No recruiting through clinicians' patients, patient lists or patient portals. No recruiting anyone you met as a patient. A clinician may share the public link with colleagues only.
4. Each of you tells your PI or programme director about Padma in writing before any outreach inside the institution.
5. No Padma work on lab time, lab equipment or institutional data. Work on a faculty-led project can belong to the university.
6. Akash uses no VUMC account, no clinical access and no clinical contacts for Padma.
7. Vanderbilt University and VUMC are separate legal entities, with separate purchasing and separate conflict offices. An answer from one is not an answer from the other.
8. If either of you is on a student visa, talk to the international student office before doing any work for pay.

## 3. Campus rules

- **Personal contacts first.** Campus channels open only after a written yes from the Dean of Students office, and only for the channel and route it names.
- **Never:** flyers in residence halls, door-to-door recruiting, commercial use of student-organisation lists. Vanderbilt bans all three.
- **Not until permitted:** posters, tables, newsletters, class group chats, any campus platform.
- **Name and logo.** No Vanderbilt logo, ever. No wording that implies endorsement. Whether the page may say "Vanderbilt students" is one of the three questions you have asked; until the answer comes, the site says "student project".
- **Email.** Interview requests go from a student's vanderbilt.edu address and say the sender is a student doing discovery interviews. If the Dean of Students says no, move to a personal address. Anything with a price goes from the Padma domain. Nothing goes from a VUMC address.
- **Activity can count as commercial even when no money changes hands.** Do not assume "it's free" makes a campus channel safe.

## 4. Never say

| Never say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| A participant count you do not have | It is false. FTC rules require true statements | "Padma is a student project in pilot stage. It has no enrolled participants yet." |
| Any number from the simulated product, in any document, email, slide or screenshot | A forwarded number loses its banner and reads as a real population | "The software runs on simulated data." Show it live, from a laptop, and send nothing |
| "Earn $X", "income", "get paid for your data" | An earnings claim you cannot keep | "Each study states its pay up front. Nothing is guaranteed." |
| "HIPAA compliant" | Padma is not covered by that law today, so the claim is meaningless and misleading | "The registry holds contact details only, and no health information." |
| "Anonymous", "fully anonymised" | Rich data can often be traced back to a person | "Records carry study codes, not names." |
| "Unhackable", "military-grade" | Nobody can promise this | "Kept in a restricted sheet with two-factor sign-in. Two named people can open it." |
| "Vanderbilt-backed", "a Vanderbilt startup" | Implies endorsement the university has not given | "Run by students. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Vanderbilt University or VUMC." |
| "Patients", "patient data" | These are research participants. "Patient data" in an email to a lab gets forwarded to the privacy office | "Research participants", "study data" |
| "Approved by the ethics board" | Padma has no approval of its own | "Each study runs under the research team's own approved protocol." |
| "100% consent verified", "FDA Part 11 grade", "CLIA/CAP", lifetime earnings | Claims from the old mockups. None is true of Padma | Nothing. They never leave the team |
| "We sell health data" | Padma holds none, and the registry is never sold | "A registry of adults willing to be invited to studies, and a service that helps research teams reach them." |
| A revenue split (90/10, 80/20) | Nothing is licensed yet, so nothing is shared | "Service fees are separate. We have no licensing revenue today." |

## 5. The money rule

- Every quote shows two lines: participant compensation and devices; Padma's service fee.
- The buyer pays participants and ships devices directly. Padma never holds participant money and never buys devices.
- Padma invoices only its service fee, only through a company with a business bank account.
- Until those exist, you sign nothing and accept no money. Scopes go out as proposals.
- No money into a personal account, ever, for any reason connected with a buyer.
- No price to a university lab until the ethics office answers.
- A Tennessee LLC costs about $700 in its first year. Form it after Gate B, not at signature.

## 6. Before any real health data is collected

All of these must be true. Today none of them is. The current software is a working model on simulated data and must not hold real health information.

**Legal and ethics**

- [ ] The company exists, and the founders have assigned their work to it.
- [ ] A lawyer has reviewed the privacy policy, the consent form and the first agreement.
- [ ] The collection runs under an approved protocol and consent: Padma's own at an independent ethics board, or a buyer's that names what Padma does.
- [ ] You have decided, with advice, which states to include. Washington, Nevada, Connecticut, Maryland and Illinois were flagged in our research.
- [ ] Adults only, with the state age rules applied.
- [ ] Consent to collect and consent to share are separate choices.
- [ ] The banned-uses list is in the agreement every buyer signs.
- [ ] There is a written plan for what happens if data is exposed, and who is told.

**Software** (from the repo audit)

- [ ] Real accounts with sign-in and a second factor. Today there is a persona switch and no accounts.
- [ ] A durable, encrypted database. Today the hosted demo loses its data when the server restarts.
- [ ] Identity kept apart from health data.
- [ ] Encrypted file storage for uploads. Today only file names and fingerprints are kept.
- [ ] Versioned consent records that show exactly what each person agreed to.
- [ ] No text sent to an outside AI service unless the consent says so and the provider has agreed in writing not to keep it. The hosted build uses the built-in rules only.
- [ ] A way to delete a person's whole account and to export it.
- [ ] Reset and other destructive controls are impossible on the real system.
- [ ] No advertising trackers, analytics pixels or session recording.
- [ ] Logs that never contain names, emails or health details.

**Money and people**

- [ ] Participants are paid by the buyer, or through a licensed payout provider. Padma holds no balances.
- [ ] Someone owns support and answers participants within a stated time.
- [ ] Insurance has been discussed with a lawyer or broker.
- [ ] For devices: the buyer's protocol covers safety and incidental findings, and the buyer ships them.
- [ ] No full phone health archives. Participants share only the named data types. Meal photos have location data removed and no faces.

There is no engineer besides the one writing this repo. Hardening the software stays parked until a pilot is signed.

## 7. Other standing rules

- **Tennessee's privacy act** does not cover a company this small. FTC rules do: true statements only, nothing shared without consent.
- **Reputation** is protected by never letting a simulated number leave the site in a document. A banner is not enough.
- **The registry collects no health information.** If someone sends some, do not store it.
- **Stop words.** If anyone replies "stop", "unsubscribe" or "remove me", they are removed that day.
- **When unsure,** do the smaller thing and ask.
