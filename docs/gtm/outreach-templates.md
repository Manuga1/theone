# Outreach templates

Copy, fill in the [brackets], read it aloud once, send. Every interview request is under 120 words; keep your personal sentence to one line so it stays that way. Log every send in the tracker the same day.

## Rules before you send anything

1. **Interview requests quote no price.** They ask for 20 minutes. They say the sender is a student doing discovery interviews.
2. **Interview requests go from a student's vanderbilt.edu address** (the plan names Maanas's). If the Dean of Students says no to that, switch to a personal address the same day.
3. **Nothing goes from a VUMC address.** Akash sends from the address agreed on the alignment call.
4. **Anything that quotes a price goes from the Padma domain**, with the opt-out line and a postal address in the footer (section E).
5. **No price goes to a university lab until the ethics office answers.**
6. **Say "research participants".** See the box below.
7. **First-week emails link to nothing**, or to the plain landing page. No attachments on a first email.
8. **One person per email.** No bulk sends, no BCC lists for interview requests.
9. **Confirm the person's current role** on their official page before writing.
10. **Metabolism and nutrition researchers are at ObesityWeek until 17 November.** Send those on Wednesday 18 November and cite their abstract.

## Do not say

| Do not say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "patients", "patient data" | "research participants", "study data" |
| "We have [any number] participants" or "our cohort" | "Padma is a student project in pilot stage. It has no enrolled participants yet." |
| "Earn $X", "get paid for your data", "income" | "Each study states its pay up front. Nothing is guaranteed." |
| "HIPAA compliant" | "The registry holds contact details only, and no health information." |
| "anonymous", "fully anonymised" | "Records carry study codes, not names." |
| "unhackable", "military-grade", "secure by design" | "Kept in a restricted sheet with two-factor sign-in. Two named people can open it." |
| "Vanderbilt-backed", "a Vanderbilt project", any logo | "Run by students. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Vanderbilt University or VUMC." |
| "approved by the ethics board" | "Each study runs under the research team's own approved protocol." |
| "We sell health data", "data marketplace" | "A registry of adults willing to be invited to studies, and a service that helps research teams reach them." |
| Any number from the simulated product | "The software you can see runs on simulated data." |
| "100% consent verified", "FDA Part 11 grade", "CLIA/CAP" | Nothing. These claims are retired |
| "We can run your study" | "We can send your approved notice to people who opted in, and report who clicked through." |
| A price, in an interview request | "I am not selling anything on this call." |

## B. Warm-intro asks (people who already know you)

Send these first. A warm ask is far more likely to become a call than a cold request.

For my discovery work I am interviewing people who run studies with research participants. The project is called Padma: a registry of adults willing to be invited to studies. It is a student project, not yet a company.

Could you help with any of these?

1. An introduction to one or two faculty who run free-living studies (wearables, sleep, nutrition).
2. A slot at legal office hours, or a mentor who is a lawyer.
3. The dates and route for micro-grants and the regional I-Corps course.

I will write the introduction text for you to forward if that is easier.

Thank you,
[Your name]
```

### B4. Your own PI: disclosure, plus two names outside the lab

I want to tell you in writing about a project I have started outside the lab with a classmate. Padma is a registry of adults willing to be invited to research studies, and a service that helps research teams reach them. It is not yet a company.

Thank you,
[Your name]

## F. Personal-contact messages for the registry

These go to adults you know personally: relatives, family friends, former colleagues, neighbours. Send them one at a time, from your own phone or personal email.

Rules:

- No income language. Do not mention amounts, earning or getting paid.
- No health questions. Do not ask about anyone's conditions, and do not pick people because of a condition you know about.
- No pressure. One message, one reminder at most.
- Not to lab members, people you mentor or teach, or anyone you met as a patient.
- Weight your list towards people over 30. The labs you want to serve mostly need adults over 35.
- Adults only: 18 or older (19 or older in Alabama and Nebraska, 21 or older in Mississippi), living in the US.

### F1. Text to a relative or friend

```text
Hi [Name], I've started a student project called Padma. It's a list of adults who are open to being invited to research studies. Joining takes two minutes and asks for contact details only, nothing about your health. You'd get an occasional email when a study might fit, and you decide each time. No promises either way, and you can leave whenever you like. Would you look? [link to /join]
```

### F2. Email to a family friend or former colleague

**Subject:** A small favour for a student project

```text

I have started a student project called Padma with a classmate. It keeps a list of adults who are willing to be invited to research studies.

Being on the list does not sign you up for anything: each study has its own consent form, and you say yes or no each time. ### Campus channels wait

Until the Dean of Students office says yes in writing, and names the route:

- no flyers or posters on campus, and never in residence halls;
- no door-to-door recruiting, ever;
- no student-organisation lists, newsletters or class group chats;
- no table at a student centre;
- no use of the Vanderbilt name or marks on anything.

A written yes covers only the channel and route it names.
